# Human resolution: a usable decision settles its finding on re-review, the jury outcome stays for calibration

A finding a human had already decided came back on every review of the same content as needing a human, so the review stayed incomplete and the gate stayed red although the question had been answered. The pipeline now reads usable decisions (docs/adr/0002-usable-decisions.md) while it reviews. Such a finding becomes a **human-resolved finding**: the human ruling outranks the jury in both directions. A true positive is accepted, and its tier and severity then meet the gate's blocking rules unchanged (a tier D finding stays tier D, so it never blocks). A false positive is not accepted, leaves the human queue, and no longer makes the review incomplete. The gate, scores, report, human queue, code-scanning results and PSIRT hand-off follow this **final outcome**.

The **jury outcome** is kept beside it, untouched. Calibration measures the pipeline, not the human. If the human ruling replaced the jury's conclusion, a finding the jury would have dropped unsupervised, but that a human confirmed as a true positive, would vanish from the rejections the Alternative Annotator Test (AAT) checks. The miss would go uncounted, and policy P8 could unlock unsupervised rejection on evidence the pipeline never earned.

A decision binds only to the content it was made on: the same target, revision and context, as ADR 0002 defines. Once the code changes, the finding goes back to a human, because a false positive in one version can be a real vulnerability in the next. Full and incremental reviews hash their context differently, so a decision made on one does not settle the other. Tool results stay out of reach: an error or warning from a deterministic tool is cleared only by that tool's own triage, never by a ticket on a model finding at the same place. A well-formed decision that is not usable (untrained, contradictory) settles nothing: the finding keeps its jury outcome, stays in the review's human queue with the reason, and keeps the review incomplete. As ADR 0002 decided, it already answers its ticket, so no new ticket is filed; governance check G-13 names the ones made by untrained adjudicators so that a trained adjudicator rules again.

## Considered options

- **Write the human ruling into the jury's fields:** rejected. Calibration and the shadow-phase baseline read `accepted` as the pipeline's own conclusion and must keep doing so, and the AAT would stop counting misses. The readers that should see the human ruling (the gate, report, queue, SARIF, PSIRT) move to the final outcome instead.
- **Carry decisions across commits** (bind by target, file, CWE and quote, as PSIRT dedupe does): rejected for now. A ruling speaks about the code it was made on, and carrying it forward risks letting a changed, now-real vulnerability through on an old false-positive ruling.
- **Let a human false positive also clear the tool result at that location:** rejected, because it would bypass the tool triage list and its recorded reasons.
